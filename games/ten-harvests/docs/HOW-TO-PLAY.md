# How to play Ten Harvests

You rule a city on the Euphrates for ten harvests. Each year you write one decree: how much land to buy
or sell, how much grain to give out as food and how many acres to sow. Then the year happens, the
scribe reports, and you decide again.

## The world and its numbers

The city has people, a store of grain and acres of land along the river. Everything you decide is paid
for out of the store, and everything the year brings in comes back into it.

| Number         | What it means                                | What moves it                                                          |
| -------------- | -------------------------------------------- | ---------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| People         | everyone living in the city                  | hunger sends people away, newcomers arrive, a fever can halve the city |
| Grain in store | every bushel the city owns                   | food, seed and land go out; the harvest comes in; rats take a share    |
| Acres          | the land the city owns                       | buying and selling                                                     |
| Acres each     | acres divided by people, shown in the ledger | both of the above; it is half of how a term is judged                  |
| Land price     | bushels an acre this year, 17 to 26          | a new price every year                                                 |

On the map every parcel is ten acres and, after dark, every lit window is ten people. The granary fills
in proportion to the store.

## One year, step by step

1. **Read the top of the tablet.** "The year before you" says what last year's harvest was, what the
   rats took and who arrived.
2. **Land.** Buy at this year's price, or sell. You can do one or the other in a year, not both, and the
   city always keeps at least one acre.
3. **Food.** Each person needs 20 bushels a year. Whoever is not fed leaves the city. If more than 45 in
   every 100 go hungry in a single year, the council meets at dawn and your rule is over.
4. **Seed.** Sowing an acre takes half a bushel of seed (an odd last acre is free), and each person can
   tend fewer than ten acres: 100 people can sow 999 acres. The small bar under the seed field shows
   which of seed, hands and land is the limit.
5. **Look at the granary bar and the Honest Ledger** (below).
6. **End the year.** There is no undo, by design. If the decree would end your rule, the button asks
   you to press it again.

Then the year plays out over the city: the flood, the sowing, the green, the harvest pouring into the
granary, the rats if they come, the newcomers on the river road, the fever if it passes. Space skips it.
The Options sheet can make it quick (three seconds) or instant.

## The granary bar

The bar under the city is the whole store, split into food, seed, land and keep, in that order. Each
handle sits between two neighbours: dragging it moves grain from one to the other and nothing else.
Food moves in whole rations of 20 bushels, seed in pairs of acres, land in whole acres. Whatever is in
"keep" stays in the granary, and that is what the rats see.

## Reading the Honest Ledger

Every number in the ledger is the one the year will use. Lines marked _exact_ are certain: who is fed,
the seed and hands. Lines marked _exact odds_ are chances stated precisely: the five possible harvests
(each equally likely, with what each would bring in), the rats (two years in five, eating a half or a
quarter of what you keep, with both amounts in bushels), the fever (one year in five). _All outcomes_
gives the full range when there are too many to list, as with newcomers. A red line at the top warns
when the decree would end your rule.

## Reading the report

The scribe's report rises over the city after each year. The headline names the harvest and the one
other thing the year will be remembered for. Below it, every event in the order it happened, with a
coloured edge: green for good, sand for quiet, amber for hard, red for a disaster. On the right, the
three numbers with their changes. New Codex entries are listed at the foot. On a replayed world a line
tells you how the earlier run stood after the same year.

## The daily world

Today's world is the same for everyone on a UTC day, numbered from launch day. Your first attempt is
the one the share line describes; replays are always allowed. Playing it keeps the Almanac streak.

## The 1978 Ledger

The teletype asks the four questions in the old order: acres to buy; only if none, acres to sell;
bushels for food; acres to sow. An impossible answer is refused with a reason and the question is asked
again. Any key hurries the printer. The rules are the original's exactly, including the way it averages
hunger, which the Codex can tell you about once you have seen it happen. A number below zero is taken
the way the original took it.

## Settings

The game's own Options (game menu, or Pause → Ten Harvests options): graphics quality (High, Balanced,
Light; the game also steps down by itself if frames run slow), the length of the year's cinematic, and
whether the camera may be turned. Everything else is in the Hall's settings: appearance, sound, reduced
motion, text size, the scribe's hints and whether the ledger's forecasts are shown.

## Endings and verdicts

A term is judged on two measures: the average share of people who went hungry each year, and acres per
person at the end.

| Ending        | When it happens                                                | Verdict                    |
| ------------- | -------------------------------------------------------------- | -------------------------- |
| Term complete | nobody went hungry in ten years, with ten acres a head or more | Ten Quiet Years            |
| Term complete | hunger at most 3 % on average, and ten acres a head or more    | A Reign Worth Carving      |
| Term complete | hunger at most 10 %, and nine acres a head or more             | A Fair Season of Rule      |
| Term complete | hunger at most 33 %, and seven acres a head or more            | A Heavy Hand on the Plough |
| Term complete | worse than that                                                | Sent Back to the Fields    |
| Deposed       | more than 45 in every 100 went hungry in one year              | The Council Has Spoken     |
| Walked out    | 1978 Ledger only: an answer below nothing                      | The Steward Walked Out     |

A Dynasty is judged on its three terms together.

## Milestones

**Stewardship:** A Full Term (10) · Worth Carving (20) · Ten Quiet Years (30) · Rain Was Not Required,
the top tier on Dry Decade (30) · Room at the Table, Crowded Gate with nobody hungry (25) · A Line of
Rulers, a whole Dynasty (35) · Not One Acre Sold, the top tier without selling land (20).

**Insight:** All Five Harvests, every yield from one to five across any runs (15) · A Full Year's
Weather, rats, fever and newcomers in one term (15) · Every Bushel Spoken For, a decree that leaves the
store empty with nobody hungry (10) · Most of the Codex (15) · The Whole Codex (30) · a secret
milestone (30).

**Curiosity:** By Teletype, a term in the 1978 Ledger (15) · The Old Way, a year through the scribe's
console (10) · Buy Low, Sell High, in Land Fever (25) · a secret milestone (10).

## Codex

Ten entries, each found by seeing it happen: The Five Harvests (what an acre gives), Rats (what happens
to grain you keep), The Fever, Newcomers (who comes, and why), The Land Market, Seed, Hands for the
Fields, Hunger, The Council at Dawn, and one more that only the 1978 Ledger shows.

## Tips for a first good run

- Sow before you feed in a lean year. An acre returns between two and ten times its seed, and a city
  with no seed has no next year.
- The rats only eat what you keep. Grain spent on food, seed or land is safe from them.
- Watch acres each. Newcomers arrive when the store is full and the land is wide, and every one of them
  lowers your acres per head.
- A fever halves the city, which raises acres per head by itself. Don't sell land in a hurry after one.
- Buy land when it is cheap (17 or 18) and only with grain you would otherwise keep.
