# How to play Crown of Florins

You rule a small Italian city-state from the year 1400, against up to five rivals in the same
valley. Climb the eight titles, from Cavaliere or Dama to Re or Regina, before your ruler's candle
burns out. The pause menu's How to play has the short version of this manual.

## The realm and its numbers

| Number       | What it means                                                   | What moves it                                                             |
| ------------ | --------------------------------------------------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| Title points | the twelve pillars added up; the heralds turn them into a title | everything below                                                          |
| Treasury     | florins; below zero it is debt                                  | taxes, courts, rents, mills; trades and building; interest                |
| Population   | serfs, soldiers, merchants, nobles and clergy                   | bread, newcomers, births and deaths, harsh courts, building               |
| Land         | hectares; the walls hold the first 5,000 for good               | buying and selling; armies take what lies beyond the walls                |
| Grain        | bushels in the granary                                          | the harvest in, the rats, trade, the bread you release                    |
| Prosperity   | the city's health, the 1978 game's "economic" figure            | building and newcomers raise it; a famine that kills over 1,000 halves it |

## A year, step by step

1. **Harvest.** The season passes over the fresco: the weather (drought, poor, fair, good,
   excellent), what the rats took (1–50 % of the granary, every year), the harvest from the fields
   your serfs could work (five hectares each, less a hundred serfs for every mill), what the people
   need and this year's prices. If your ruler died over the winter, the heir's year begins here.
2. **Market.** Buy or sell grain at this year's price (florins per 1,000 bushels) and land at this
   year's price per hectare. Grain is dear when the harvest is short of what the people need, and
   land with it. You cannot sell more grain than you have, nor land inside the walls. Buying beyond
   the treasury is allowed: it is debt.
3. **Bread.** Release grain to the people: at least a fifth of the granary, at most four fifths. The
   gauge marks what they need and the 130 % that draws newcomers. Less than the need and some go
   hungry; at 130 % or more families come to the gates, with merchants, a noble and two clergy. The
   shortcuts buy just enough grain first when the granary is too small.
4. **Taxes and justice.** Customs, sales and wealth taxes share one willingness to pay (150 less the
   three rates), so raising one lowers what the others bring in. The seal of justice runs from very
   fair (1) to outrageous (4): the courts cost (300 × seal − 500) × your title in florins a year
   (very fair costs money, harsh brings it in), harsh and outrageous courts drive up to 1 % and 4 %
   of serfs away, and the seal is taken straight from your title.
5. **Builders.** A marketplace (1,000 fl: five merchants and 75 fl rent a year), a woollen mill
   (2,000 fl: 56–305 fl a year, a hundred serfs at the looms), a piece of the palace (3,000 fl: one or
   two nobles), a piece of the cathedral (5,000 fl: one to six clergy, six stages from foundations to
   bell tower), or a platoon (500 fl: twenty serfs armed, on guard from next year).

Then **seal the year**. If the plan would leave many hungry, spend past the creditors' line or leave
the border watch red, the game asks once. There is no undo after the seal.

**Title review.** A herald unrolls the scroll: what happened, what the treasury took in and paid
out, what the people did, and the verdict. A promotion is lettered in gold.

**The valley.** Between years the fresco becomes a map of the valley. Every city at its true size,
its banner rising with its title points; armies that marched this year march again along the roads:
the column as long as its army, the garrison formed before its walls, the clash of pennants and
dust, the result lettered on a ribbon and the loser's banner dipping.

**When a rival is crowned.** The first realm to reach Re or Regina wins the race. If it is a rival,
the heralds tell the whole table at once and offer two ways on: play on for a crown of your own
(it counts as "A Crown Worth the Wait"), or end here and read the chronicle. From then on the step
bar carries the news ("Roccaventosa crowned Regina Selvaggia, 1443"), the winner wears a crown in
the rival bar, and the pause menu keeps "Close the chronicle". Rivals crowned later are marked as
crowned later; they never end your game.

## The glass box

Everything the engine uses is on the right of the year screen, computed by the same rules as the
year itself. Every card fits above the rival bar: the road, the border watch and the debt share
one card of tabs (a red dot on "Border watch" when an army will march, on "Debt" when you owe), as
the ruler and the realm do on the left; a long panel scrolls inside its card and says "more below"
while it does.

- **Preview of the year**: births, deaths and newcomers, what each tax and the courts will bring,
  the mills, and the title the review would give, by name ("Granduchessa → stays", "→
  Principessa"), as the range that held in eight of ten trial years (the real year uses its own
  dice, never these).
- **The treasury**, told one way everywhere: _after this plan_ (exact: the treasury less this
  year's trades and works) and _expected at the year's end_ (after rents, the mills, soldiers' pay,
  the taxes, the courts and any interest). Hover or focus the second for the year's money line by
  line.
- **Road to the crown**: the twelve pillars as bars, each worth up to ten points (one per
  marketplace, palace piece, cathedral piece and mill; one per 5,000 florins, 6,000 hectares, 50
  merchants, 5 nobles, 50 soldiers, 10 clergy, 2,000 serfs and 5 of prosperity), and the points
  needed for the next title. Gold cells are this year's works. The title is points ÷ (difficulty
  - 5), less the seal of justice, plus one, rounded down. Titles never fall.
- **Border watch**: an army marches on you this year if you have fewer soldiers than thousands of
  hectares (after this year's land trade), or if a rival's published army is more than 2.4 times
  yours. It is certain, not a gamble, and the watch says so in advance. The strongest rival able
  to spare its troops marches and takes its soldiers × 1,000 hectares less a third of its own land;
  if none can, mercenaries take 1,001–10,000. The walls keep the first 5,000.
- **Debt**: a negative treasury grows by half every year. Below −10,000 florins per step of title,
  the creditors take every building, half the merchants, all but 6,000 hectares and 4,000 bushels,
  and leave 100 florins. (They look at the books before the builders are paid, so works bought on
  credit bring them the year after.)
- **Lifetime**: your ruler's age, the years the rule allows, the chance of dying this year and of
  living five more. The year itself is hidden; the odds are exact. The physician frowns as the
  odds rise, and the candle in the portrait burns lower.

Forecasts can be hidden in the collection's settings, for the 1978 fog.

## Lifetime and dynasty

A ruler's last year is fixed on taking the throne: between 21 and 55 years later, every year equally
likely. Two years before the end the heralds raise the ruler one title, whatever the points say.

- **Dynasty** (the default): an heir takes the throne one title lower, with the city intact. Any
  ruler of the line may be crowned. If a Cavaliere or Dama dies with the treasury in debt, no heir
  comes forward and the line ends.
- **Classic fate**: as in 1978, death ends that seat's game.

## Rivals

Up to five AI rivals, each a real ruler under the same rules, seeing only what you see:

| Rival        | How they rule                                                                |
| ------------ | ---------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| the Merchant | markets and mills, low customs, a full purse                                 |
| the General  | the biggest army in the valley, harsh courts, a long look at weak neighbours |
| the Builder  | saves for the palace and the cathedral                                       |
| the Miser    | high taxes and the fullest treasury                                          |
| the Saint    | very fair courts, generous bread, a growing clergy                           |

Difficulty also sharpens them: an Apprentice's rivals have an off year one time in three, a Grand
Master's never.

## Modes

| Mode            | What it is                                                                                                                                                                           |
| --------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------ |
| Begin (solo)    | you and three rivals by default; the court setup lets you seat one to five                                                                                                           |
| Hot-seat        | two to six people at one device, with AI rivals if you like; a velvet curtain hides each realm until its ruler is ready, and other people's numbers stay hidden until the year's end |
| Daily           | the same valley, court, weather and lifespans for everyone on a UTC day; the first attempt counts for the share                                                                      |
| Scenarios       | five valleys of our own (below)                                                                                                                                                      |
| Endless Dynasty | no end date: the crown is a chapter and the line rules on until you close the chronicle (pause menu)                                                                                 |

Every other game ends at the first human crown, at the end of your line, or when the century turns
in 1500. A rival's crown does not end it: you choose to play on or to end there.

## Settings

Crown of Florins' own options (pause menu, or Settings on the game menu): graphics (high, balanced,
light; the fresco also steps down by itself if your computer struggles), year speed (cinematic,
quick, instant), the valley between years on or off, and how rivals' marches show in hot-seat (each
one, or all together). The collection's settings hold appearance, sound, reduced motion, text size,
hints and forecasts.

## Tips for a first good run

- Feed everyone first. A hungry year costs serfs, merchants and prosperity, and the shortcut on the
  Bread step buys exactly the grain you need.
- Markets early: they pay for themselves in about thirteen years and bring merchants who pay
  customs.
- Keep an eye on the strongest army in the rival bar. A platoon a year is cheaper than the land an
  army takes.
- Harsh courts pay, but every seal is a whole title's worth of points at review. Very fair justice
  in the year you are close is often what tips the scroll.
- Watch the candle. When the odds of the end rise, spend on what counts for the title now.
